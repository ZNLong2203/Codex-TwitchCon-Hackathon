# Codex x TwitchCon: research and concept assessment

Research checked 30 October 2026, Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh. Facts below link to their sources; recommendations and predictions are explicitly labeled as assessment. The event overview and official rules were also supplied in the task attachments and checked against the live pages.

## Entry and judging facts

- [Official rules](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/rules) specify a newly created interactive experience for Twitch streamers and their community, built with Codex and connected to Twitch (examples include chat, overlays, Twitch API, EventSub, or Extensions). Work submitted must be made during the submission period; frameworks, libraries and starters are permitted. Original work, ownership, third-party licenses and permissions matter.
- Deadline is **2 November 2026, 2:00 p.m. Pacific** (PST, UTC−8), which is **3 November, 5:00 a.m. Vietnam time**, and 2 November 22:00 UTC. The [FAQ](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/resources) also says 9:00 p.m. UTC, which conflicts with its stated Pacific and ET times. Use the event countdown and submit early; verify with organizers if necessary.
- Submit repository URL (GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket), complete source/assets/run instructions, project description with Codex use, session ID from `/status`, and a YouTube demo under three minutes showing the project working on Twitch and how it was built with Codex. Private repos must be GitHub/GitLab shared with testing@devpost.com. Rules say publicly visible; FAQ explicitly permits unlisted, not private. English or complete English translations are required. Project must be free and unrestricted to judges through the judging period. Judges may judge video, text and images without running code. [Rules](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/rules), [FAQ](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/resources).
- Pass/fail theme and developer tool viability precedes four equally weighted criteria: innovation, potential impact, technological implementation including Codex, and design. Top five become finalists. The prizes are $10,000, $6,000, $4,000, and two at $2,500. Attendance at TwitchCon is not required. [Rules](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/rules), [FAQ](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/resources).
- The FAQ says a paid Codex plan is unnecessary; free usage has limits. Neither rules nor FAQ requires an AI model at runtime, a specific model, a spending budget, or a particular quota. Codex is mandatory as a build tool. Fees or availability for optional services depend on their actual providers and plans. Do not promise a zero-cost hosted deployment merely because the runtime needs no AI API.
- New and original are separate from globally unprecedented: the rules require original entrant work, and the judges assess novelty relative to existing concepts. If entering multiple projects they must be unique and substantially different. Do not reuse the example ideas as complete products. The [gallery](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/project-gallery) was unpublished when checked, so current submissions could not be compared.
- Individuals must be at least 18 or local age of majority, whichever is higher; excluded jurisdictions, employees and conflicts are specified in the rules. Check actual eligibility rather than infer it from location. The FAQ says Extensions may take 5–14 days for review, and unreviewed Extensions cannot be installed by judges; chat and browser overlays are faster paths. The FAQ advises freezing linked submission materials after the deadline.

## Prior evidence and competitive landscape

The following are confirmed awards, not generic project-gallery entries. The [2024 Twitch Streamer Tools Hackathon winner announcement](https://twitchstreamertools.devpost.com/updates/33853-streamer-tools-hackathon-2024-winners-and-runner-ups) names:

- [Stream Together Timeline](https://devpost.com/software/stream-together-timeline): Streamer-to-Streamer Collaboration winner; records collaborations and clips for shared timelines.
- [PixelWars](https://devpost.com/software/bitwars): Interactive Community Experiences winner; viewer-tagged characters compete across communities or against a streamer-selected tower.
- [tcghud](https://devpost.com/software/tcghud-by-tangia): Enhancing Stream Appearance winner; browser-assisted card identification and information overlay.

The same announcement identifies Spectators Control Minecraft Mobs and Lights Out! as runners-up. **Assessment:** successful precedents have visible live consequences and focused use cases, but those traits do not establish a formula for winning. PixelWars is particularly close to any chat-controlled character game; a new overlay alone is not credible novelty.

[Avatar Raiders](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1895040/Avatar_Raiders/) already connects to Twitch chat and supports transparent broadcast overlays. [Twitch's chat tooling](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/) already offers the underlying chat primitives. **Assessment:** avoid marketing chat commands, avatars, point accumulation, or a generic Twitch Plays mechanic as unique. Any rescue concept needs its teamwork mechanics, story, and newcomer participation to be demonstrable and meaningfully distinct. Existing product availability and features can change; this is not an exhaustive landscape survey.

## Three feasible concepts (assessments, not claims of uniqueness)

1. **Raid Relay — cooperative rescue overlay (recommended).** Streamer starts a short rescue mission. Viewers join different roles that perform complementary actions in timed phases; a new arrival sees the current objective and can make an immediately useful move. A clean OBS browser source visualizes cause and effect while the streamer retains start, pause and reset control. Minimum viable architecture: deterministic server-side mission state, command ingestion from Twitch, shared local dashboard and browser overlay. The novelty hypothesis is the combination of complementary roles, rescue handoffs and late-arrival catch-up; it must be validated by live play and is not a claim to an unprecedented genre. Risk: without interdependence it collapses into a counter, or resembles PixelWars/Avatar Raiders. Design a moment where one role's action enables another, visibly record the handoff, and demonstrate with real viewers.
2. **Live Context Cards.** Streamer and chat collaboratively maintain approved, compact context cards for viewers arriving late in science, creative, or tabletop streams; streamer approves proposals and can show a scene-specific recap overlay. Deterministic templates avoid unsupervised AI output. Risk: moderation workload and an undifferentiated shared note; must demonstrate meaningful newcomer catch-up and safe acceptance controls. Feasible with chat ingestion, approval queue, and overlay.
3. **Co-op Production Cues.** During a maker or tabletop stream, a streamer opens a real-time cue board; viewers propose safe, structured options and the streamer activates a collaboratively assembled scene or challenge, with role-specific contributions. Distinct from a generic poll only if several contributions are combined into a visible outcome, rather than majority voting. Risk: lacks immediate spectator payoff without polished transitions and creator-specific use cases. Feasible as chat command processing plus streamer controls and overlay.

No research can guarantee a prize. Select one concept, demonstrate a working end-to-end loop, and test with a real stream rather than add unsupported claims of novelty.

## Twitch integration evidence and practical implications

- [EventSub chat authentication](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/authenticating/) describes registration and token/scopes; [subscription types](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/eventsub-subscription-types/) specify `channel.chat.message` and its conditions. Minimum read permissions depend on the chosen token and transport; a local user-token approach needs `user:read:chat`. Ask only for necessary scopes, do not commit tokens, and validate the chosen account/channel configuration. A read-only experience does not inherently need write permission.
- [WebSocket handling](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/eventsub/handling-websocket-events/) states that a subscription must normally be created within 10 seconds of welcome. Unexpected disconnection requires a new session and resubscription and events lost during disconnection are not replayed. Handle reconnection, deduplicate message IDs as appropriate and accurately surface connection failure. Test actual reconnect behavior; simulation is not evidence of live connectivity.
- [Chat rate limits](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/) apply independently of normal API limits. Regular users who are not broadcaster/mod/VIP are limited to 20 sent messages per 30 seconds and one per second per channel; privileged senders have a 100-per-30-second bucket. The API's send-message bucket is distinct, and exceeding chat limits can cause suppression. Avoid responding once per incoming command; a read-only overlay avoids bot output spam.
- Browser-source overlays should share authoritative mission state, survive refresh and show legible connection status. Filter exact commands, bound input, deduplicate/cool down viewers, render text safely, and protect streamer controls. This is engineering advice, not a Twitch promise. Never expose credentials in the repository, screenshots, overlay URLs, or public demo.
